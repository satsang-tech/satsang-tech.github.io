Keepsake

Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 10, 2026

This policy applies to the Keepsake Android app, package app.keepsake.gallery, developed by Satish Ratanchand Ranka, Nagpur, Maharashtra, India, and published on the Satsang Technology website. It describes the app's current data practices.

Your photo library is processed on your phone. Keepsake does not upload your photos, videos, face representations, recognized photo text or contact labels to us. There is no account, advertising, publisher analytics or cloud photo service.

Place-name lookup is the online exception. Reverse geocoding is enabled by default and may send saved photo coordinates to your phone's geocoding provider. You can switch it off in Library settings → Place names → Use offline names.

1. Photos, videos and local records

With your permission, Keepsake reads media made accessible by Android, including filenames, capture dates, dimensions, file sizes and available photo metadata. It uses this information to display your library, albums, timelines and maps, play videos, find duplicates and support editing.

Private app storage holds library references, favorites, album membership, captions, keywords, ratings, local date/location adjustments, edit recipes, Hidden status, indexing progress and settings. Keepsake also keeps thumbnails and temporary working data for browsing and processing. Original media remains in your device's shared media library; it is not moved into a private cloud.

2. Face grouping and text recognition

Bundled models detect faces and compute numerical representations of facial features to group similar faces into anonymous clusters. These representations can be sensitive biometric information. Face positions, cluster identifiers, optional names, contact links, chosen covers and your merge/separation corrections are stored locally. The app also reads text in photos for search and computes fingerprints for duplicate suggestions.

Photos, face representations and recognized text are processed locally and are not sent to us or a remote AI service for recognition or model training. Recognition may be wrong; you can correct groups and names. A face group or the “Me” label is an organizational feature, not proof of identity or a security credential.

Automatic indexing is enabled by default and can run in the background, with limits based on charging, battery and temperature. It can be paused or its results erased using the controls below. Hidden photos are excluded from new face/text indexing and duplicate suggestions; hiding a previously indexed photo does not itself erase its saved results.

3. Contacts and your phone profile

Contacts permission is optional. When you type a person's name, Keepsake queries matching display names and contact identifiers from Android's contacts provider. Selecting a suggestion saves the name and a local contact reference with that person. You can use a plain text label without linking a contact.

If you choose “Link my phone contact,” Keepsake attempts to read the owner profile exposed by Android. Marking someone as “Me” does not automatically identify the phone owner. Keepsake does not modify your address book or upload it. Revoking Contacts permission stops new lookups but does not erase names or links you already saved; remove those links or erase people data to remove them.

4. Photo locations and online place names

With photo-location permission, Keepsake can read GPS metadata already embedded in accessible photos. It saves coordinate cells rounded to two decimal places and uses bundled offline map and town data. It does not request live device-location permission or continuously track your phone.

By default, while the app is open and a network is available, Keepsake also requests city, town or region names through Android's system geocoder. Requests provide saved coordinate cells and the device language/locale. Photo files, face representations, recognized text, names and contact information are not included in those requests.

The geocoding provider depends on your device and may process requests online, including associated connection information such as your IP address, under its own privacy policy. We do not operate that provider or control its retention. Keepsake itself has no direct Internet permission; that does not prevent Android's geocoding service from making network requests on its behalf.

Use Library settings → Place names → Use offline names to stop further lookups and use bundled names. This does not recall requests already sent. Successful local lookup results are reused for up to 30 days; expired entries may remain until cache eviction or app-data removal. Hidden status does not by itself disable coordinate lookup.

5. Sharing, copying and editing

When you choose Share, Android passes the selected media to the app or recipient you select. Their handling is governed by their own policies. Sharing originals can disclose embedded metadata, including location where Android permits access. Edited-copy export offers separate choices for camera/capture metadata and GPS. Check those choices before sharing.

Copy places media references on Android's clipboard. Duplicate and export create files in shared media storage, where other apps with permission can access or back them up. Removing Keepsake does not remove these files, your original photos or copies already shared elsewhere.

6. Permissions, background work and diagnostics

  • Photos and videos: browsing and processing accessible media. Android may allow all-media or selected-media access.
  • Photo location: reading original embedded GPS metadata and original bytes for relevant media operations. This is distinct from live location tracking.
  • Contacts: optional name suggestions and owner-profile linking.
  • Notifications, foreground service, wake lock and boot events: visible Index now progress and resuming scheduled indexing under Android's controls.
  • Network state: checking connectivity before the optional online place-name lookup.

The app reads battery, power-saving and thermal status to limit processing. Android's screen-lock confirmation protects access to Hidden and Recently Deleted; Keepsake receives an authentication result, not your PIN, password or system biometric templates.

Keepsake writes local technical logs, such as processing counts, timings and errors. It has no automatic crash-reporting or analytics upload service. Android, Google Play, your device manufacturer and apps you choose to share with may separately handle installation, security or diagnostic information under their policies.

7. Your controls, retention and deletion

  • Pause face recognition or erase people data: Library settings → Indexing & privacy. Erasing removes saved face representations, groups, names, manual people tags and contact links, and pauses face indexing. Original photos remain.
  • Pause or erase text/duplicate results: Library settings → Text & duplicates. Erasing these results also pauses that indexing and leaves photos and other details intact.
  • Manage permissions: use Android Settings → Apps → Keepsake → Permissions. Restricting access does not itself erase previously saved private metadata.
  • Remove all app records: clear Keepsake's app storage/data in Android settings or uninstall it. This removes local app records and caches, not original media, exported/shared copies or information already received by an external provider.
  • Delete media: on Android 11 and later, supported deletion moves media to Android's trash with confirmation. Recently Deleted lets you recover or permanently delete accessible trashed files; Android controls expiry. On Android 10, deletion is permanent and the app warns you.

App-created records are retained until erased through the relevant controls or app-data removal. Some metadata can remain after a source photo is removed, including to support recovery. Restarting recognition after erasing results can create new results from photos you still allow the app to access. There is no Keepsake account or server-side library for us to delete remotely.

8. Storage security and backups

Local records use Android's private app storage. Keepsake does not provide a separately encrypted photo vault. Hidden changes visibility within Keepsake; other apps may still see originals, and clearing Keepsake data removes its Hidden markings.

Keepsake disables Android app backup and supplies rules excluding its app data from cloud backup and device transfer. Device and operating-system behavior can vary. Your original media and exported files remain subject to any separate backup service you use. No app can guarantee protection on a compromised or unlocked device.

9. Photos of other people and children

Your library may contain other people, including children. Their faces and any labels are processed in the same local way described above. Use these features and share photos only when you are entitled to do so, with any consent required for your circumstances. Keepsake does not create remote profiles of those people or use their information for advertising.

10. This website and support requests

This policy page is hosted on GitHub Pages. Loading it sends ordinary web-request information, such as your IP address, browser information, requested page and request time, to GitHub to deliver and secure the page. The page contains no advertising or analytics scripts. See the GitHub Privacy Statement.

If you email us, we receive your email address and the information you provide through our email service. We use it to respond and retain it only as needed for the request and applicable obligations. We do not sell your personal information. We cannot remotely retrieve the photo library or recognition records stored on your phone.

11. Changes to this policy

We will update this page and its “Last updated” date when our practices change. Material new uses of personal information will receive appropriate notice and, where required, consent before they begin.
